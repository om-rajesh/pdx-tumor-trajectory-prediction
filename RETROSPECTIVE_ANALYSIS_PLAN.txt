DEPTH AND DURABILITY OF RESPONSE IN PATIENT-DERIVED XENOGRAFTS
Analysis plan

1. OBJECTIVES

Using serial tumor volumes from patient-derived xenografts (PDX): relate depth of response to post-nadir regrowth (durability); test whether two regrowth mechanisms are identifiable on the source study's schedules; test whether early trajectory features beat depth for forecasting day-24 volume and selecting treatments; explore molecular, combination and mechanism-class correlates; and use external PDX cohorts (measurement error, between-animal variation) and patient trials (prognostic value of trajectory shape).


2. DATA AND POPULATIONS

Primary data: Novartis PDX Encyclopedia workbook (Gao et al., 2015). An arm is one PDX model-treatment pair, usually one animal. Optional or restricted: PDXNet volumes, Oberg et al. ovarian tumor areas, RNA-seq and hallmark gene sets, comparator arms of NSCLC trials A6181087 and CA031 (Project Data Sphere). Sections lacking their inputs are skipped and marked not computed.

Post-nadir arms (durability analyses): nadir after the first measurement and followed by at least two. Primary forecasting population: post-nadir arms with a complete day-10 window and an observed day-24 volume. Broader populations are sensitivity analyses.


3. PROCESSING

Check the workbook's SHA-256; normalize treatment labels before building arm IDs. Set volumes recorded as zero or below 1 mm³ to 1 mm³. Average same-day replicates on the log scale. Recompute baseline, nadir and durability and count disagreements with the helper module. Fix all seeds, one generator per analysis. Record software, checksums, seeds, constants and run time in a manifest; stop if a required consistency check fails.


4. ENDPOINTS

Depth: the source study's best average response, the minimum from day 10 of the running average percentage change from baseline.

Durability: least-squares slope of log volume after the nadir, nadir excluded (as the minimum of noisy readings it is biased low); with two post-nadir readings, their two-point slope.

Relationship: Spearman correlation, with intervals and P values from resampling PDX models and a two-way bootstrap over models and treatments. Sensitivity: arm duration, window, tumor type, treatment, untreated-growth adjustment, number of post-nadir points, nadir inclusion.

Discordance: within a PDX model, a pair of arms with positive post-nadir slopes is depth-matched if best average response differs by under 5 points and discordant if slopes differ by at least 0.02 per day. Thresholds are varied; the share expected from measurement noise alone is computed on each pair's schedule. Further analyses restrict to responding pairs and to arms held at the floor.


5. MECHANISTIC MODELS

Exponential growth, constant kill, tolerant subpopulation (a fraction grows at the untreated rate) and declining effect (kill decays exponentially); the last two are non-nested competitors. Analyses: approximation of one regrowth model by the other on study schedules; local identifiability (condition numbers, including relative sensitivities); parameter recovery under the stated priors (noise SD 0.15 log units, arms ended above 2,000 mm³, bounded least squares from three starts; recovered if median absolute log error is below 0.25, also reported when (k − g) × span ≥ 1); a design map (150 replicates per design, common random numbers); AIC comparison, unpaired and paired; prior predictive checks and simulation-based calibration of a Bayesian tolerant-subpopulation model not fitted to data.


6. GROWTH, NOISE AND TERMINATION

Untreated growth: slope of log volume in untreated arms with at least five measurements. Measurement noise: median residual SD of those fits, with pooled and interpolation estimates as checks. Kill rates: tolerant-subpopulation fits with growth fixed at the model's untreated rate (three starts); variance partitioned descriptively and by crossed REML (model-by-treatment interaction inseparable from residual). Termination: logistic regression of last-visit probability on standardized log volume and elapsed day, bootstrapped over arms, giving censoring weights (probabilities floored at 0.05) for post-nadir growth summaries.


7. FORECASTING AND TREATMENT SELECTION

Forecast day-24 log volume from readings up to day 10. The outcome is interpolated between positive readings, without readings below 1 mm³; predictors use the floor. Arms observed under 24 days are excluded from the primary analysis.

Depth set (baseline log volume, best average response in the window) and trajectory set (depth plus eight shape descriptors) are fitted by the same gradient-boosted regressor (300 iterations, learning rate 0.05, L2 penalty 1.0, 15 leaves) on five folds grouped by PDX model. A leakage check perturbs readings after day 10 and requires every predictor to stay unchanged.

Endpoints: relative reduction in out-of-fold RMSE (margin 10%) and in mean regret (margin 15%), where regret is the selected arm's observed day-24 log volume minus the best observed in its PDX model. Intervals: 2,000 resamples of PDX models, predictions fixed. An endpoint is met when its margin is reached and its 95% interval excludes zero (Holm across the two); whether each lower bound clears its margin is also reported, with one-sided P values (Holm). Endpoints and margins were set before the first forecasting run; the eligibility rule and learner settings were fixed after it.

Ranking accuracy scores tied predictions as one half and drops pairs with tied outcomes. Control sets, other windows, held-out tumor types, censoring scenarios, other populations, separation strata, baseline plus slope, strongest comparators, other learners, fold partitions and leave-one-out analyses are robustness or exploratory analyses.


8. MOLECULAR CORRELATES

Signatures (EMT, stemness, cell-cycle arrest) from within-model gene percentile ranks; context features: stromal score, baseline log volume, untreated growth, tumor type. Population: post-nadir arms with at least five measurements and expression and untreated-growth data (gastric models have no expression data). Ridge regression on five folds grouped by PDX model, penalty by inner grouped cross-validation, scored by out-of-fold R². Null: 500 shuffles keeping tumor-type structure, P = (1 + null values ≥ observed) / 501. Dissociation statistic: signature-minus-context R² for durability minus that for depth, interval from 2,000 resamples of PDX models. Hallmark and treatment-adjusted versions are robustness analyses.


9. COMBINATIONS AND MECHANISM CLASS

Combinations: evaluated only where every component was tested alone in the same PDX model, as gain over the best component (positive favours the combination). Categories: depth gain above 5 points, durability gain above 0.005 per day. With at least eight evaluations: two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank for depth, two-sided sign test for durability (some gains are infinite), Benjamini-Hochberg across combinations. Deeper: q < 0.05, positive median. Slower regrowth: q < 0.10, most evaluations favouring the combination. Adverse effects reported separately.

Mechanism class: single agents grouped into nine families from the source annotation, dose variants pooled. Planned contrast: cytostatic (cell cycle, PI3K-mTOR, MAPK) against cytotoxic and apoptosis-inducing families, predicting slower regrowth and shallower depth. Cross-family pair differences are averaged within each PDX model, then across models. Planned tests: 10,000-replicate bootstrap over PDX models and 10,000 family-label permutations. Compound-level, mixed-model and other randomization analyses are sensitivity analyses.


10. EXTERNAL AND CLINICAL ANALYSES

External: PDXNet animals with at least four readings over at least five days; between-animal SD of growth in model-arm groups of three or more, corrected for slope error and floored at zero, untreated groups primary. Measurement error from interpolation residuals, resampling series, untreated and treated separately; Oberg areas separate, converted to volume only under the stated scaling. Also: variance components, regret against a simulated second animal, discordance expected with between-animal variation.

Clinical: comparator arms of A6181087 and CA031. RECIST 1.1 progression: at least 20% and 5 mm above the running minimum (incomplete assessments cannot set it), or a new lesion. Landmark at the second scheduled assessment (day 112 and day 84); cohort assessed at or after it, progression-free before it, with at least two complete earlier assessments. Predictors: depth (1 − minimum SLD / baseline SLD), final-interval log-SLD slope, and a rising indicator. Cox model with depth alone against depth plus shape; out-of-fold Harrell's C on five folds stratified by event (and study when pooled); paired difference from 2,000 patient resamples.

Single-trial plan (A6181087): primary plus four sensitivity analyses (lowest reading removed, nadir clock, sum only, baseline-adjusted), Holm across five. Pooled plan, written after the single-trial primary result: pooled primary, leave-one-study-out, each study, sum only, baseline-adjusted, nadir clock, Holm across seven. Added after both plans: harmonized new-lesion definition, third-assessment landmark, investigator-assessed outcome, nonlinear depth comparator.


11. MULTIPLICITY AND REPORTING

95% intervals resampling each analysis's independent unit (PDX model, treatment, patient, series or paired replicate). Holm for the two forecasting endpoints and within each clinical family; Benjamini-Hochberg across combinations. Report estimates with intervals, sample sizes, exclusions, optional-input status, failed and boundary fits, and undecidable model comparisons. Later and exploratory analyses are secondary, never presented as confirmatory or prospective. Patient-level clinical files stay local.


12. DEVIATIONS AND LIMITATIONS

Changes from the first clinical analysis, all affecting results: running-minimum RECIST reference, exclusion of pre-landmark progression, handling of zero and incomplete lesion readings, nadir-clock predictor timing, censoring at new lesions in sum-only analyses, within-study concordance when pooled. Added later: clinical extensions, BRAF V600 subgroups of combinations and encorafenib, further robustness analyses.

Limitations: one animal per arm, confounding measurement error with between-animal variation; termination at large volumes; few long schedules; limited identifiability; simulations conditional on assumptions; restricted external and clinical data; forecasting and selection populations defined from the complete trajectory, so the all-treated analyses are needed to interpret them.


13. REPRODUCIBILITY
